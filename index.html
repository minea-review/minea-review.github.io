What Minea is actually selling

Strip the landing page down and Minea is an ad library with a product-research skin on it. The pitch is that somewhere inside a very large pile of running ads there is a product already converting for someone else, and that copying it beats guessing. The site advertises more than 80 million active ads and a user base north of 200,000 dropshippers, with a 4.7 out of 5 rating displayed next to it. Those are the vendor's own numbers, shown without a source, so treat them the way you would treat any homepage statistic. What matters more is the shape of the tool: search ads, filter them, look at the shop behind them, then act. Everything else on the page is a convenience layer bolted onto that loop.

The database, and the filters that make it usable

A pile of ads is worthless without filters, and this is where the product earns its subscription. Minea lets you narrow by spend, by how long an ad has been running, and by activity level, which is the closest public proxy anyone has for whether a product is actually selling. The shop side goes further: traffic estimates, revenue estimates, the top-performing ads for that store, and the apps and tools the store runs on. That last detail is genuinely useful, because a store's app stack tells you how it handles upsells, reviews and shipping without you having to reverse-engineer the checkout yourself. The estimates are estimates. Treat them as ranking signals for a shortlist, not as accounting.

Convenience features that sound better than they are

Three extras get top billing. A daily list of ten trending products, a one-click path from a product you like to a Shopify store, and a screenshot lookup that finds every creative tied to a product you photographed. The daily list is the weakest of them, for the obvious reason that every other subscriber sees exactly the same ten products on the same morning. By the time a product is on a curated daily list it is not a secret, and your margin has already been priced in by the people who moved first. The store builder saves setup hours, not strategy. The screenshot lookup is the one I kept using, because it turns a competitor's ad into a full creative history in about a minute.

Pricing is the part nobody publishes

You will not find a plan table on the homepage. There is a free entry point, prominently placed, and a Chrome extension, and a dropshipping course sold alongside the software, but the actual monthly cost lives behind a signup. That is a deliberate choice and a common one in this category, and it means any price you read on a review site, including this one, could be stale before you finish reading it. Check the official page for current rates rather than trusting a number in a blog post. Budget for the course upsell too, since the site treats training and tooling as one funnel and the course has its own page in the main navigation.

Who should skip it

Skip it if your products are not ad-driven. An ad library only helps when the market you sell into buys from paid social, and plenty do not. Skip it if you already have a supplier relationship and a catalogue, because you are paying for discovery you no longer need. Skip it too if what you actually want is to watch five named competitors closely rather than trawl millions of ads you will never look at twice, since a broad library makes that narrow job harder, not easier. And if the real bottleneck is finding people to make the creative, a database of finished ads does not solve it. That is the gap Reachara was built for.

What lands in your account

A searchable ad library

Running ads from public libraries, filterable by spend, activity and how long each one has been live. This is the core of the product and the reason most people keep paying.

Shop-level estimates

Traffic and revenue figures for stores behind the ads, plus their top creatives and the apps they run. Useful for ranking a shortlist, not precise enough for real forecasting.

A one-click Shopify push

Turn a product you found into a store without leaving the tool. It saves setup time, which is the cheapest part of the job, so weigh it accordingly when comparing plans.

A browser extension

A Chrome add-on that lets you pull data while browsing stores and feeds rather than switching tabs into the dashboard. Small feature, but it changes how often you use the rest.

How to judge it in one afternoon

  1. Pick three products you already know
    Search for products you have sold or watched closely. If the library misses ads you know were running last month, coverage in your niche is thinner than the headline number suggests.
  2. Stress the filters, not the search
    Anyone can return results. Filter by spend and by days active, then check whether the survivors are genuinely different from the unfiltered list. That difference is what you are paying for.
  3. Cross-check one shop by hand
    Take a single store, note the traffic and revenue estimates, then compare them against any public data you can find. You are calibrating how far to trust the numbers later.
  4. Price the whole stack before you commit
    Add the subscription to your ad spend, your creative costs and any course upsell. Then ask whether a narrower tool such as Reachara covers the part you actually use daily.

Questions people ask before subscribing

Is Minea worth it for a complete beginner?

It depends on what is stopping you. If you cannot decide what to sell, an ad library gives you a starting shortlist and that has real value. If your problem is creative, fulfilment or ad account approval, the tool does not touch any of those, and a subscription becomes a monthly reminder of a bottleneck it cannot fix.

How much does Minea cost?

The homepage does not publish a plan table, so any figure quoted elsewhere may be out of date. There is a free entry point you can use to look around first. Check the official site for current rates before budgeting, and remember the dropshipping course is sold separately from the software itself.

Are the traffic and revenue numbers reliable?

They are estimates built from public signals, like every tool in this category. They are good enough to rank ten stores against each other and put three on a shortlist. They are not good enough to justify a large inventory order, and no vendor in this space, including the ones that publish methodology, claims otherwise.

Do the ten daily winning products actually win?

Sometimes, but every subscriber sees the same list on the same morning. That is fine if you move quickly and have creative ready. If your launch cycle takes two weeks, you are entering a market that has already been discovered, priced and saturated by faster operators using the identical list.

What is the best alternative to Minea?

It depends which half you need. For finding products to dropship, competing ad libraries cover similar ground. For watching a defined set of competitors and finding creators to make your ads, Reachara at reachara.com handles that narrower job without the product-research layer you may never open.

Can I cancel easily?

Cancellation terms are set out in the vendor's own terms of service, not here, and they change. Read them in your account settings before the first renewal date rather than after it. If you started on the free entry point, confirm what happens to saved searches and lists when a paid plan lapses.
